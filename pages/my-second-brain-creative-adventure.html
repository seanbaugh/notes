My Second Brain Creative Adventure

And how I found amazing ways Claude could help me in my actual work.

SB
Sean Baugh
Apr 24, 2026 · 4 min read
🧠

I'm a tech junkie anyway, so these ideas have always fascinated me. I also waste far too much time exploring these ideas, experimenting with things, then leaving them behind as they just didn't seem to serve me in ways I expected.

Well, I might have discovered (well, not MY discovery, but a personal discovery nonetheless) how emerging tech CAN help me in my daily creative work.

And yes, it involves AI.

And yes, I know about all the concerns. I also share those concerns. I also would love to have a debate with you (at another time) how some of those concerns are misplaced, IMO. So, we are going to leave those behind, for now, as AI is here, and it's here to stay. And if we don't figure out moral ways to use it, we might be surprised when it bites us in the ... well .....

I digress.

Two tools, one idea

There are two tech interests I've developed lately — the "note-taking application" Obsidian, and the AI agent Claude.

I have been working on building a "second brain" for some time now, first with Notion (which, is fantastic, but I found Obsidian to be more attractive) — a place where I could literally store every idea, thought, resource, plan, in one central place that could be easily accessible, searchable, and discoverable. Obsidian fit the bill for me.

So, I started to build and follow practices that Obsidian is meant for — linking notes, similar ideas, tags, etc — so it is all connected.

This is cool! It's also a rather manual process. Tags make things easier, but it's still manual.

Great ... enter Claude.

A real-world test

I'm a new user to Claude. And I have already discovered amazing uses in my creative work. For example, just today, I was trying to brainstorm ideas for our upcoming cabaret, and frankly genres I wasn't familiar with. So, I just asked Claude!

I asked: "I'm producing a cabaret featuring solo singers called 'Songs We Can't Sing on Stage.' It basically is a concert that will feature songs that are a bit risqué, funny, slightly sexual, eyebrow-raising, fun, silly — all the things a sophisticated choir wouldn't perform. It will be a casual and fun event. What are some songs I can program? Can range from old songs, to Broadway, to new pop songs — can be anything really. Give me some ideas!"

And it delivered — instantly. This saved me hours of work. It didn't DO the creative work FOR me, only introduced me to things I didn't know yet.

And yes, I could use it to build a program for me — and I might — but this is great for now.

So, I popped the results into my Obsidian notes, and planned to refer to later.

When things clicked

Later that day, I ran across this video and things just CLICKED:

"I Built an AI Second Brain Using Obsidian + Claude (Copy Me)"

I followed his instructions, and in under an hour — and after feeding Claude only TWO articles — just TWO! — Claude had built a TTBB Repertoire Wiki for me, which will only grow with new content added. I simply asked Claude to add the article to my wiki and it did the work in Obsidian, creating connections, links, descriptions, etc.

You can see the result here: Sean Baugh Notes — Obsidian Publish

That's from just TWO articles!

The moment that floored me

Then things got even more interesting. I asked Claude simply, "What pieces in my wiki would be good for my upcoming September 11 concert" — and it gave me a thoughtful, curated answer drawn entirely from my own notes.

I'm dumbfounded.

But also excited — because one of the most difficult tasks I have as a concert programmer is keeping track of potential repertoire. This goes right at the heart of that problem.

The honest take

Will this experiment be cast upon the heap of fun projects down the road? Maybe.

But you cannot deny how powerful and useful this can be for creatives.

And the best part is — it's not doing the creative work for us — only giving us new tools to do our work more efficiently.

I'll report back later with more discoveries!
